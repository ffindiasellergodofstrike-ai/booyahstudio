## 2. Digital-goods rule

Because products are delivered electronically and may become accessible immediately after verified payment, purchases are generally non-refundable once the purchased product has been successfully delivered, accessed or downloaded. A customer accessing or downloading the product generally establishes digital delivery. This rule is subject to applicable law and the legitimate exceptions below; it is not an absolute refusal of refunds.

## 3. When a refund may be appropriate

We reasonably review requests involving: successful payment with no purchased access or delivery; a confirmed duplicate payment for the same order; a wrong product supplied because of our error; materially corrupted or technically unusable files where reasonable assistance cannot resolve the issue; a product materially different from its description at purchase; a refund required by applicable law; or another legitimate case approved after review. We may offer a corrected file, replacement or restored access where that appropriately resolves the issue without limiting a legal refund entitlement.

## 4. Cases that do not automatically qualify

Changing your mind, disliking a design after seeing the delivered product, not knowing how to use the technology, or failing to review stated requirements does not automatically create a refund entitlement, subject to law. For example, a React template accurately described as requiring development knowledge is not automatically refundable because the buyer expected a hosted service. A listing that wrongly promised an included backend presents a different issue and will be reviewed as a possible description mismatch.

You can stop an unpaid checkout before completing payment. Closing the payment window does not establish that an attempted payment failed. Check the order if your account was debited.
